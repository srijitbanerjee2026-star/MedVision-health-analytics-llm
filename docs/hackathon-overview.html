ER triage, read by machine, decided by clinicians.

MedVision Guard takes a patient's vitals, runs them through both hand-written clinical rules and a trained risk model, and hands the care team a triage read, a likely-diagnosis breakdown, and a recommended length of stay — in under a second, before a doctor ever opens the chart.

FastAPI + React LightGBM risk model 3-step intake MIT-style ER workflow
01

The problem

An ER intake desk sees a constant stream of patients whose vitals alone — oxygen saturation, heart rate, blood pressure, temperature — already carry most of the signal for how urgently they need to be seen. But that first read is usually a nurse doing mental math under time pressure, and it doesn't get written down anywhere structured until much later.

MedVision Guard is a fast, honest first pass: type in the vitals, get a structured triage read back immediately — acuity level, a ranked guess at what's going on, and a resource plan (admit or observe, how often to check in, how long they'll likely stay). It's built to sit in front of a clinician, not to replace one.

02

How it works

Three steps, from a blank form to a structured care plan.

1
Enter vitals, in three short screens
Core vitals (SpO2, heart rate, BP) first — the essentials for a fast read — then additional vitals (resp rate, temp, pain score), then medical history and free-text findings. Each step validates before letting you move on.
2
The backend scores it three ways
A rule-based triage level (1–5) and disease-probability breakdown run instantly on thresholds a clinician could sanity-check by eye. In parallel, a trained LightGBM model scores real risk from the full 13-field vector and turns that into a care plan.
3
One dashboard, built around the diagnosis
The predicted condition is the headline — not buried under a severity gauge. Everything else (probability breakdown, risk score, vitals, recommended stay) is supporting detail arranged around it.
03

05

The AI layer

Two models exist in this repo. We're upfront in the product itself about which one is real.

Risk model Live · in production

A LightGBM binary classifier predicting high-risk vs. lower-risk from the full 13-field vitals vector (age, SpO2, heart rate, resp rate, both BP readings, temp, pain score, and five history flags). Its probability output becomes a 0–100% risk score, which a set of tiered thresholds turns into a disposition, a monitoring cadence, and an estimated length of stay.

Type
Binary classifier
Inputs
13 features
Output
Risk % → care plan
Trained on
Pre-existing dataset
Severity model Demo · synthetic data

An XGBoost 5-class severity classifier, built to show the training pipeline works end-to-end even without a real dataset on hand. train_xgboost.py seeds a local SQLite database with synthetic vitals and independently-random acuity labels — so this model is honest infrastructure, not a clinical signal, and it's deliberately not wired into the UI.

Type
5-class classifier
Trained on
Synthetic, unlabeled-real
Accuracy
~28% (no real signal)
Status
Backend only
